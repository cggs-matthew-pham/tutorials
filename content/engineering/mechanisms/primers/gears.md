## The idea in plain words

Two gears that mesh push each other round, tooth by tooth. The gear that is turned by the motor is the **driver**; the gear it turns is the **driven** gear. The **gear ratio** is driven teeth ÷ driver teeth.

When a small gear drives a big one (ratio more than 1), the big one turns **slower** but with more **turning force**, by the same factor: 8 teeth driving 24 is a ratio of 3, so the output turns 3 times slower and can lift 3 times as much. Swap them round and it turns 3 times faster but is 3 times weaker. Nothing is free: a gear trades speed for force.

A design brief sets **criteria** (what it must do: lift 450 g) and **constraints** (limits: within 15 s, with this motor and these gears). A design passes only if it meets every criterion. Sometimes no design does, and the constraints have to be talked about.

## What students get wrong

- **A bigger gear is faster.** A bigger driven gear turns less for each turn of the driver.
- **The ratio upside down.** Driven goes on top.
- **Only one thing changes.** Speed and force both change, in opposite directions.
- **Checking one criterion.** A design that lifts the load but is too slow fails the brief.

## The words

**gear**, **driver**, **driven gear**, **gear ratio**, **turning force (torque)**, **stall**, **criteria**, **constraints**, **trade-off**.
