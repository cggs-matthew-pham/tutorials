  ## About

  Waypoints tutorials are short coding lessons that run in your browser, with maths and science lessons on the way. There's nothing to install and no account to make, and we don't track anyone's progress beyond their own browser.

  Each idea is explained in a few different ways, because students come at things differently. You can try the code yourself, watch a short clip with captions (the sound is optional), look at a picture of what the code makes, or read an explanation in plain words. Cheat sheets and worksheets print for times away from the screen. Every lesson also has notes for the teacher, written for someone who may never have taught coding before.

  The lessons follow the Australian Curriculum (version 9) and are meant to be done in order, though you're welcome to start wherever suits you.

  ## Why we made this

  A lot of coding in schools is taught by teachers who aren't programmers themselves, often in short units squeezed between other topics. Coding builds on itself more than most subjects do, so a student who misses one idea can quietly fall behind on everything that follows. We wanted a path that keeps the steps small and in order, keeps track of what each lesson builds on, and gives teachers what they need to run it with confidence.

  Teachers can [suggest or request a lesson](https://forms.gle/CoRAXJWHnVQ8mXpPA), and each one is reviewed before it's added. Part of [Learning Waypoints](https://learningwaypoints.com).

  ## How the lessons work

  Students read code before they write it. Each lesson follows **PRIMM**, an approach developed by Sue Sentance and colleagues and used in schools in the UK and beyond: students *predict* what a short program will do, *run* it to see if they were right, *investigate* how it works, *modify* it, then *make* something of their own. Now that AI can write code, being able to read a program and tell whether it's right matters more than ever, and that's the habit these lessons try to build.
