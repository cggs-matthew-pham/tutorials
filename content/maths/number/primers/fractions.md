title: Fractions and decimals on the line in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

Every fraction, decimal and mixed numeral has one place on the number line. To find it, split each unit into equal parts (quarters for $\tfrac{3}{4}$, tenths for $0.6$) and count from zero: right for a positive, left for a negative.

A negative mixed numeral or decimal is a whole number and a part, **both** below zero: $-1.5$ is one unit left of zero and then half a unit further left, so it sits between $-2$ and $-1$. Different names can share a place: $-\tfrac{2}{4} = -\tfrac{1}{2} = -0.5$.

## Why it matters

Scales on thermometers, rulers and graphs are number lines in parts. Ordering mixed fractions and decimals, and later adding and subtracting them, all rest on knowing where each one sits.

## What students get wrong

- **Counting the part back towards zero.** −1.5 placed at −0.5, between −1 and 0. Ask whether −1.5 °C is colder or warmer than −1 °C.
- **Comparing decimals by digits.** −0.3 judged greater than −0.25 because 3 < 25. Write both with the same number of places.

## The words

**fraction**, **decimal**, **mixed numeral** ($1\tfrac{3}{4}$): ways of writing numbers between the whole numbers. **equivalent**: two names for the same number, at the same place on the line.
