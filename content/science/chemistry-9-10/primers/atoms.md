## The idea in plain words

The particles of Years 7 and 8 have parts. An atom has a tiny, dense **nucleus** of **protons** (charge +1, mass 1) and **neutrons** (no charge, mass 1), with **electrons** (charge −1, mass about 1/1840) around it.

- The **atomic number** is the number of protons. It decides the element.
- The **mass number** is protons plus neutrons. Neutrons = mass number − atomic number.
- A neutral atom has as many electrons as protons; an **ion** has gained or lost electrons.
- The model changed because of evidence: Thomson's electrons, Rutherford's gold foil (a tiny positive nucleus), Chadwick's neutrons.

## Where the picture breaks

- The board's Bohr model is not to scale: the nucleus would be about 100 000 times smaller than the atom.
- Electrons are drawn as dots on rings; they are better described as clouds where they are likely to be.

## Common misconceptions

- The mass number is the number of neutrons.
- Electrons carry a lot of the mass because they fill the atom.
- Adding neutrons changes the element (it makes an isotope).
